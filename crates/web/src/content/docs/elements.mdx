An element supplies measurement, drawing, and input behavior. The tree owns its state and lifetime. `Tree::add` attaches a new element. Moving it preserves state; removing it drops its descendants and callbacks.

## Built-in elements

- `Container` groups children.
- `Panel` draws a border around its content, in a `Border` style, with an optional
  title. A background color in its style fills it, which makes it opaque over
  whatever it overlaps.
- `Text` draws a string.
- `RichText` draws styled spans with optional word wrapping.
- `Input` edits a single line. `mask` hides a secret, `limit` caps its length,
  and `cursor` chooses the cursor's shape.
- `Textarea` edits multiple lines and can soft-wrap them.
- `List` and `Table` draw only their visible rows and share their navigation:
  arrows, paging, Home, End, the wheel, and clicks. `List` rows are styled spans.
  Their view moves only when the selection leaves it, so a click never slides
  the rows under the pointer.
- `Scroll` clips its children to a viewport and paints only those in view, and
  scrolls to show a child that receives focus.
  `Scroll::with_bar` adds a scrollbar in a column of its own.
- `Feed` shows a long document of styled text blocks, such as a log or a
  conversation.
- `Lazy` shows a long column of element subtrees, such as messages with their
  own chrome, and lays out only those in view.

## Long documents

A `Scroll` holds elements, and layout visits every one of them, so a frame costs
more as the content grows. A `Feed` holds text blocks instead and lays out only
the blocks in view: a frame costs what is visible, however many blocks there are,
and replacing the last block with `Feed::set` relays out only that block. In the
local timing example, a streaming frame over 10,000 wrapped blocks takes about
0.03 ms in a `Feed` and about 4 ms in a `Scroll`.

A feed follows its tail until the user scrolls away, and holds its place while
blocks arrive beneath. Its position is a block and a row within it, not a row
count, because the rows above the view are never measured.

When each entry is a subtree of elements, such as a message with a header and
controls or an entry of a file tree, add the entries as children of a `Lazy`. The
tree lays out only the children in view, and keeps each child's layout until
something in it changes. It scrolls, reveals focus, and follows its tail with
`Lazy::follow` the way a feed does. In the same timing example, a streaming
frame over 10,000 wrapped `Text` children takes about 0.05 ms in a `Lazy`. A
feed stays the faster choice for text alone, because its blocks are not elements.

For the same reason neither has a height of its own: each grows into the space
its parent gives it. A frame of natural height, such as an inline session, gives
it none, so set a height in its layout there.

## Editing

Both editable elements use `wove::text::Editor`. Cursor positions are byte offsets
on grapheme boundaries.

- Shift extends a selection; Ctrl+Z undoes an edit; Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redoes it.
- The multiline editor accepts pasted newlines; a single-line input joins pasted lines with a space.
- Moving vertically through short lines preserves the display column.
- With `Textarea::wrap`, Up and Down move through the wrapped rows.
- A click places the cursor, Shift-click and dragging select, and a click in a
  `List` or `Table` selects the row under it.

Edits are data. A `Command` inserts text, moves by a `Motion`, deletes up to a `Motion`, selects all, undoes, or redoes; `Editor::apply` runs one. `text::command` maps an event to a command under the default bindings: arrows, Home and End, Ctrl or Alt with arrows for words, Ctrl+W and Alt+Backspace to delete a word, Ctrl+K and Ctrl+U to delete to the end or start of the line. Applications with their own bindings handle the key first, in their loop or a node handler, and apply the commands they choose.

An atom is a run of text that acts as one unit, for tokens that stand for something
else, such as a collapsed paste. `Editor::insert_atom` adds one with an
application-chosen id. The cursor steps over it, an edit that touches it removes
it whole, and undo brings it back. Atom boundaries always include whole graphemes.
If an edit joins neighboring atoms into one grapheme, they merge under the
leftmost atom's id. A new atom that joins existing text or atoms includes them
under its own id. Undo restores the previous text and atom identities.
`Textarea::atom` styles atoms.

Undo keeps at most 100 edits and stores changed text rather than a copy of the entire document. A typed word and the whitespace after it are one edit. Text storage is currently a `String`; large-document editing still needs profiling.

## Custom elements

Implement `wove::Element` and add your type to a tree. Drawing goes through `Canvas`, which clips output to the assigned area, expands tabs, and removes other terminal control characters. `render::columns` counts the cells a line of text takes the same way, and `render::fit` cuts text to a number of cells, so an element measures what it paints. An element with nothing visible is not painted, and `Canvas::visible_rows` tells a tall element which of its rows to draw. A scrolling element implements `Element::reveal` to show a descendant that receives focus. Just before painting, `Element::viewport` receives the element's inner size, so an element settles what it shows, such as its first row or a scroll offset, and then paints exactly that. Mouse events arrive with positions relative to the element's top-left cell, so an element can act on a click without knowing where it was laid out or scrolled to. `Element::overlay` draws over the element's children, which is how `Scroll` draws its bar. No global registration is required.

Any element, custom ones included, can be a child of a `Lazy`. The column guarantees its children the following:

- Children stack from the top in child order; `z` does not reorder them.
- Each child is laid out on its own, as the only child of a flex column as wide as the `Lazy`'s content box, and only when it comes near the view, is scrolled past, or holds the focus.
- A child that is not in view is not painted, has empty bounds, and cannot take a click. It stays in the focus order, and focusing it scrolls it into view.
- A child's layout is kept until the child or a descendant changes through `Tree::update`, `Tree::set_layout`, or an event response that asks for layout.

In return, a child takes its height from its content at the width it is given. Percentage heights and `flex_grow` have no height to fill, and a child with nothing to measure takes no rows. A child that changes how it measures must say so through one of those paths, as any element must, or the column keeps its old height.
